# Diving Back In, Part Two: Choosing Swim Gear

## 1. Warm-up

Summer exercise can be tricky when it is too hot to jog outside, so the hosts talk about swimming as a practical option. In the dialogue, Tom checks his old swimsuit and goggles before getting back into swimming, and Lisa reminds him not to buy low-quality gear.

여름에는 밖에서 뛰기엔 너무 더울 수 있어서, 진행자들은 수영을 좋은 운동으로 이야기합니다. 대화에서는 Tom이 다시 수영을 시작하려고 낡은 수영복과 수경을 확인하고, Lisa가 장비는 품질을 너무 아끼지 말라고 조언합니다.

---

## 2. Expressions

**1. way too**
- Much more than is acceptable, comfortable, or reasonable
- 너무, 지나치게, 말도 안 되게 ~한

**2. beyond saving**
- Too damaged or worn out to be repaired or made useful again
- 고칠 수 없을 만큼 망가진, 도저히 살릴 수 없는

**3. skimp on**
- To spend too little money, time, or effort on something important, usually lowering the quality
- ~에 돈이나 노력을 너무 아끼다, 대충 때우다

## 3. Power Dialogue

**Tom:** Okay, I tried on my old swimsuit this morning. Definitely way too tight.
**Lisa:** Time to pick out a new one then.
**Tom:** And I need goggles; these are beyond saving; scratches everywhere like looking through fog.
**Lisa:** Get antifog ones. They're worth the extra money. Don't skimp on quality for those.
**Tom:** Good point, I'm actually getting really excited about this.
**Lisa:** Nice! Let me know how it goes. I want details.
**Tom:** Will do. Wish me luck.

## 5. Notes

### way too
- Use **way too** before an adjective when something is much more extreme than it should be. It usually sounds negative or uncomfortable, not like a simple compliment.
- **A:** Is this backpack okay for hiking?
  **B:** No, it is way too heavy. Your shoulders will hate you.
- The meeting was way too long, so everyone lost focus.

### beyond saving
- If something is **beyond saving**, it is too damaged, old, or worn out to fix. You usually have to replace it or give up on repairing it.
- **A:** Can we clean these old shoes?
  **B:** No, they are beyond saving after all that mud.
- The old sofa was beyond saving after the basement flooded.

### skimp on
- To **skimp on** something means to spend too little money, time, or effort on it. It often leads to lower quality, especially when the thing is important.
- **A:** Should I buy the cheapest helmet?
  **B:** No. Do not skimp on safety gear.
- Do not skimp on good shoes if you run every day.

---

## 6. Power Pattern

### will do
- Used as a short, friendly response meaning "I will do that" or "I will take care of it."
- 알겠어, 그렇게 할게

**Examples:**
1. "Can you send me the file tonight?" "Will do."
2. "Remember to bring a towel." "Will do."

---

## 7. Power Vocabulary

### antifog
- Designed to prevent fog or clouding, especially on goggles, glasses, windows, or lenses.
- 김 서림 방지의, 안티포그 처리된

**Examples:**
1. These antifog goggles stay clear even during a hard swim.
2. I bought an antifog spray for my glasses before the ski trip.
