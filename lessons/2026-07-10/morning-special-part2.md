## 2. Today's Idiom
- Idiom: work like a dog
- Korean meaning: 뼈빠지게 일하다, 아주 열심히 오랜 시간 일하다

### Setup
- 두 진행자가 놀이공원에서 아르바이트하는 대학생 역할을 맡는다. Ray는 커다란 다람쥐 인형 탈을 쓴 직원이고, 최수진은 동화 속 공주로 분장한 직원 Sue를 연기한다.

### Dialogue
**Sue:** Excuse me, Mr. Squirrel! You dropped a brochure.

**Ray:** Welcome to Moss Adventure Island.

**Sue:** Um, excuse me, Mr. Squirrel?

**Ray:** Oh, sorry. I can't hear very well in here. How can I help you?

**Sue:** Well, you dropped these brochures back there. Here you go.

**Ray:** Thank you so much. I didn't even notice. Hey, I was actually just about to take my break. Would you like to join me at the staff resting area behind that big tree over there?

**Sue:** That'd be lovely. Thank you. Could you hold these brochures for a second?

**Ray:** Sure. Whoa, boy! It is hot inside this thing. Hi, I'm Ray, or the friendly squirrel of Moss Adventure Island.

**Sue:** Nice to meet you, Ray. I'm Sue, or Princess Mondarella, at your service.

**Ray:** It's an honor to meet you, Your Highness. Good thing you don't have to wear a furry costume. It's absolutely sizzling today.

**Sue:** I know. How can you stand the heat wearing that?

**Ray:** Well, the costume actually has a built-in ventilation and cooling device inside. It usually keeps the temperature mildly cooler, but on days like today, it's of no use at all.

**Sue:** I have to say, that sounds really bad.

**Ray:** Yeah, but it's okay. I get to take ten-minute breaks every half an hour, with all the water and ice I want to drink for free.

**Sue:** I guess that's reasonable. My job is just to greet children, take photos with them, and be as regal and friendly as a princess can be. I get my breaks every hour—not bad. So, are you a student around here?

**Ray:** Yes, I'm at Miwa Women's University, majoring in finance. What about you?

**Sue:** I'm from Maist University, majoring in engineering.

**Ray:** An engineer. I see. That explains why you understand the costume's cooling system so well. Is this your first time working at the park?

**Sue:** Yeah, it's my first time. Yours too?

**Ray:** Yes, it is. Have you done other part-time jobs before?

**Sue:** A few, but mostly retail. How about yourself?

**Ray:** I've worked at a cafe. I did some tutoring as well last summer, but I have to confess, I like this one more than I thought I would.

**Sue:** I totally agree. It's tough, but also rewarding. Plus, food and transportation are included, and we get to meet new people and make friends along the way.

**Ray:** Exactly! I really like the experience so far. I might even consider working on a cruise ship next summer.

**Sue:** That sounds great! I'd love to try something like that too, but I bet that's an even tougher gig than this one.

**Ray:** It is. You have to work like a dog for eight weeks straight, dealing with angry passengers, carrying stuff up and down the decks, and getting seasick every now and then. But it's really worth it, especially when you get to travel to different countries.

**Sue:** Yeah, it kind of sounds like a dream gig. Why don't we get together at lunch? I'll tell you more about it.

### Meaning
- To work very hard, often for long hours.
- 아주 열심히, 대개 오랜 시간 동안 일한다는 뜻이다.

### Host Explanation Notes
- 현대의 반려견 이미지와는 다르게, 과거에 개가 작업 동물로 쓰이던 데서 나온 표현으로 소개했다. 정확한 기원 시기는 진행자도 추정으로만 언급했다.
- 영어에서는 단순히 일이 아주 많고 바쁘다는 뜻으로 자연스럽게 쓸 수 있다.
- 한국어로 직역하면 다소 거칠게 들릴 수 있으므로, 문맥에 따라 “뼈빠지게 일하다”나 “정말 열심히 일하다”로 옮기는 편이 자연스럽다.

### Example Usages
- The construction crew worked like a dog in the pouring rain to finish repairing the highway before the morning rush hour.
- I have been working like a dog this week.
- You've been working like a dog recently.

### Similar Expressions
- be busy as a bee
- have one's nose to the grindstone
- work oneself to the bone
- burn the candle at both ends

## 3. New York Times Article
- Title: Do I Really Need to Wear Sunglasses Every Time I Go Outside?
- Topic summary: 외출할 때마다 선글라스를 써야 하는지, 일반 처방 안경도 자외선을 막을 수 있는지, 그리고 눈과 눈 주변을 보호하려면 무엇을 확인해야 하는지를 안과 전문가들의 설명으로 다룬 기사.
- Why it matters: 자외선은 날씨와 관계없이 시력 관련 질환과 눈 주변 피부 손상의 위험을 높일 수 있다. 다만 투명한 일반 안경에도 자외선 차단 기능이 있을 수 있으므로, 렌즈의 차단 성능을 확인하고 필요에 따라 선글라스·모자·자외선 차단제를 함께 쓰는 것이 중요하다.

### Key Article Passages
1. Shielding your eyes from sun exposure is, as far as experts are concerned, not up for debate. “Sunglasses are sunscreen for your eyes,” said Laura Demelio, an assistant professor of ophthalmology at the Wilmer Eye Institute at Johns Hopkins Medicine. They help protect your eyelids, the skin around your eyes, and the surface of your eyes.
2. Sunlight contains three types of ultraviolet radiation: UVA, UVB, and UVC. But only the first two reach the Earth's atmosphere. UVB radiation is believed to be more dangerous for your eyes than UVA; however, both are present whether it's sunny or not, and both can increase your risk for conditions that affect your eyesight.
3. The area around your eyes is vulnerable too; five to ten percent of skin cancers in the United States develop on the eyelid. Even if you're protected from UV rays, which can accelerate signs of aging, bright light can make you squint, which contributes to wrinkles.
4. Your clear prescription frames can have as much protection as dark lenses. Modern eyeglass lenses are often made from polycarbonate, which has UV protection built in, and non-polycarbonate lenses often come with a special coating that provides UV protection.
5. Whether you're shopping for sunglasses or eyeglasses, look for a label that says UV 400 or 100% UVA/UVB protection. These terms are essentially synonymous; they mean the lenses block 99 to 100 percent of ultraviolet light.

- Transcript note: The spellings of Laura Demelio, Moss Adventure Island, Princess Mondarella, Miwa Women's University, and Maist University follow the transcript and may be uncertain.

## 4. Vocabulary and Useful Sentences

### be up for something / not be up for debate
- Meaning: to be available for consideration or discussion; if something is not up for debate, it is considered settled
- Korean: 논의·검토의 대상이다 / 논쟁의 여지가 없다
- Useful sentence: Shielding your eyes from sun exposure is not up for debate.
- Sentence Korean: 햇빛 노출로부터 눈을 보호해야 한다는 사실은 논쟁의 여지가 없다.
- Note: `up for debate`와 `up for discussion`은 의견을 나눌 수 있다는 뜻이고, `not up for debate`는 이미 사실로 받아들여져 더 논쟁할 필요가 없다는 뜻이다.

### sunnies / shades
- Meaning: informal words for sunglasses
- Korean: 선글라스
- Useful sentence: But do people who wear eyeglasses need a pair of sunnies too?
- Sentence Korean: 그런데 안경을 쓰는 사람도 선글라스가 하나 더 필요할까?
- Note: `sunnies`는 영국·호주 영어에서, `shades`는 북미 영어에서 흔히 쓰는 비격식 표현으로 설명했다. `shades`는 선글라스를 뜻할 때 복수형으로 쓴다.

### have all one's bases covered
- Meaning: to be prepared for every likely situation and not miss important details
- Korean: 모든 경우에 대비하다, 만반의 준비를 갖추다
- Useful sentence: Are you sure we have all of our bases covered before this meeting?
- Sentence Korean: 이번 회의 전에 우리가 모든 경우에 철저히 대비한 것이 맞나요?
- Note: 야구의 베이스 수비에서 유래한 표현이다. 기사 속 `some of your bases are covered`는 어느 정도 보호되지만 완전한 보호는 아니라는 뜻이다.

### ultraviolet (UV)
- Meaning: radiation beyond the violet end of visible light
- Korean: 자외선
- Useful sentence: Sunlight contains three types of ultraviolet radiation: UVA, UVB, and UVC.
- Sentence Korean: 햇빛에는 UVA, UVB, UVC라는 세 종류의 자외선이 포함되어 있다.
- Note: 진행자들은 `UV`가 `ultraviolet`의 줄임말이라고 설명했다.

### cataract
- Meaning: a condition in which protein buildup clouds the lens of the eye
- Korean: 백내장
- Useful sentence: Cataracts—a protein buildup that clouds the lens of the eye—can occur naturally as we age.
- Sentence Korean: 눈의 수정체를 흐리게 하는 단백질 축적인 백내장은 나이가 들면서 자연스럽게 생길 수 있다.
- Note: `cataract surgeon`은 백내장 전문 외과의를 뜻한다.

### cloud
- Meaning: to make something less clear or harder to see, understand, or judge
- Korean: 흐리게 하다, 불분명하게 만들다
- Useful sentence: Cataracts—a protein buildup that clouds the lens of the eye—can occur naturally as we age.
- Sentence Korean: 눈의 수정체를 흐리게 하는 단백질 축적인 백내장은 나이가 들면서 자연스럽게 생길 수 있다.
- Note: 눈의 수정체가 뿌옇게 되는 물리적 상황뿐 아니라 `cloud the issue`, `cloud your judgment`처럼 이해나 판단을 어렵게 한다는 비유적 뜻으로도 쓴다.

### progressive
- Meaning: gradually becoming worse over time, especially of a disease or condition
- Korean: 진행성의, 점차 악화하는
- Useful sentence: Alzheimer's disease is a progressive brain condition.
- Sentence Korean: 알츠하이머병은 점차 악화하는 뇌 질환이다.
- Note: 의학 문맥에서는 증상이 그대로 머무르지 않고 시간이 지나며 계속 나빠진다는 뜻이다.

### cornea / refractive eye surgery
- Meaning: the transparent front part of the eye / surgery that corrects how the eye focuses light
- Korean: 각막 / 굴절 교정 수술
- Useful sentence: Almost every cell type is found within and around the eye, said Dr. Dagny Zhu, a cornea, cataract, and refractive eye surgeon based in California.
- Sentence Korean: 캘리포니아에서 활동하는 각막·백내장·굴절 교정 수술 전문의 Dagny Zhu 박사는 거의 모든 종류의 세포가 눈과 눈 주변에 존재한다고 말했다.
- Note: 진행자들이 기사에 나온 안과 관련 전문 용어의 한국어 뜻을 짚어 주었다.

### squint
- Meaning: to partly close one's eyes, especially because of bright light or difficulty seeing
- Korean: 눈을 가늘게 뜨다, 눈을 찡그리다
- Useful sentence: I squinted in the bright sunlight.
- Sentence Korean: 햇빛이 너무 강해서 눈을 가늘게 떴다.
- Note: 빛이 너무 밝을 때뿐 아니라 멀리 있는 글자나 아주 작은 글씨를 잘 보려고 눈을 좁힐 때도 쓴다.

### crow's feet
- Meaning: small lines at the outer corners of the eyes
- Korean: 눈가 잔주름
- Useful sentence: Wearing sunglasses helps relax your eye muscles, which prevents things like crow's feet.
- Sentence Korean: 선글라스를 쓰면 눈 근육이 이완되어 눈가 잔주름 같은 것을 예방하는 데 도움이 된다.
- Note: 주름 모양이 까마귀의 발처럼 보이는 데서 나온 표현이며, 보통 복수형으로 쓴다.

### built in / built-in
- Meaning: included as part of something from the beginning rather than added later
- Korean: 내장된 / 붙박이의
- Useful sentence: Modern eyeglass lenses are often made from polycarbonate, which has UV protection built in.
- Sentence Korean: 현대식 안경 렌즈는 자외선 차단 기능이 내장된 폴리카보네이트로 만들어지는 경우가 많다.
- Note: 명사 뒤에서는 `built in`, 명사 앞에서 수식할 때는 `built-in camera`처럼 하이픈을 넣어 쓴다.

### optical / optician / optometrist / ophthalmologist
- Meaning: relating to eyes or vision / a person who supplies and fits glasses / an eye-care professional who examines eyes and writes prescriptions / a medical eye specialist who can perform surgery
- Korean: 눈·시각의 / 안경사 / 검안사 / 안과 전문의
- Useful sentence: If you want to be certain that your glasses have sun protection, you can bring them to a local optical shop to have them checked.
- Sentence Korean: 안경에 자외선 차단 기능이 있는지 확실히 알고 싶다면 가까운 안경점에 가져가 점검받을 수 있다.
- Note: 진행자들은 `optician`은 처방에 따라 안경을 맞추고, `optometrist`는 눈을 검사해 처방하며, `ophthalmologist`는 수술도 하는 전문 안과 의사라고 구분했다.

### a fraction of
- Meaning: a very small part or amount of something
- Korean: 아주 작은 일부, 극히 일부분
- Useful sentence: I was going to buy this car, but then I decided to buy a slightly used car for a fraction of the price.
- Sentence Korean: 이 차를 사려고 했지만, 나중에는 가격의 일부만 내고 약간 중고인 차를 사기로 했다.
- Note: `fraction`은 수학에서 “분수”이지만, `a fraction of something`은 전체 중 아주 작은 부분을 강조할 때 자주 쓴다.

### be synonymous with
- Meaning: to have the same meaning as something, or to be very closely associated with it
- Korean: ~와 동의어이다, ~와 밀접하게 연관되다
- Useful sentence: K-pop is synonymous with Korea.
- Sentence Korean: K-pop은 한국과 떼려야 뗄 수 없는 관계다.
- Note: 단어의 뜻이 실제로 같을 때뿐 아니라, 하나를 들으면 다른 하나가 바로 떠오를 만큼 밀접한 관계에도 쓴다.

## 5. Final Main Expressions Recap

1. be up for something
- meaning: to be available for discussion, consideration, or competition
- example: The championship is still up for grabs.
- Korean: 논의·검토·경쟁의 대상이다; 아직 결정되지 않았다

2. cover all the bases
- meaning: to prepare for every likely possibility and take care of every important detail
- example: Are you sure we have all of our bases covered before this meeting?
- Korean: 모든 경우에 대비하다, 만반의 준비를 하다

3. progressive
- meaning: gradually becoming worse over time, especially of a disease
- example: Alzheimer's disease is a progressive brain condition.
- Korean: 진행성의, 점차 악화하는

4. squint
- meaning: to partly close the eyes because of bright light or difficulty seeing
- example: I squinted in the bright sunlight.
- Korean: 눈을 가늘게 뜨다, 눈을 찡그리다

5. be synonymous with something
- meaning: to have the same meaning as, or be closely associated with, something
- example: K-pop is synonymous with Korea.
- Korean: ~와 동의어이다, ~와 밀접하게 연관되다
