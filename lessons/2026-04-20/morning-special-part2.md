## 2. Today's Idiom

- Idiom: **to have a field day**
- Korean meaning: 아주 신나게 즐기다 / 어떤 상황이나 기회를 마음껏 이용하다

### Setup
셰인이 배우, 최수진이 매니저 역할. 셰인의 첫 레드카펫 행사 준비 중, 스타일리스트가 준비한 차콜 슬림핏 정장 대신 친구가 보내온 네온 색상의 80년대 Miami Vice 스타일 과대 사이즈 수트를 입겠다고 해 매니저를 경악시키는 상황.

### Dialogue
**Sue (Manager):** Please stop fidgeting. If the artist smudges that concealer, we're ten minutes behind schedule.
**Shane (Actor):** I can't help it, Sue. My first red carpet! Look at my hands; they're actually shaking.
**Sue:** Okay, deep breaths — you look like a star. Okay? Oh, makeup is done — go and get dressed. The car's downstairs.
**Shane:** Oh, speaking of getting dressed — I have a huge surprise.
**Sue:** No, no, no — not another surprise, please, Shane. You know I don't have room for surprises when it comes to the public eye. Everything has been perfectly choreographed for a reason.
**Shane:** Relax, Sue. This time it's different. A friend of mine owns a tailor shop and he sent me this summer suit that is perfect for today's event.
**Sue:** What? No! What happened to the designer outfit? The stylist sent over last night — the charcoal slim fit.
**Shane:** Oh, I'll wear that some other day. It was a bit stiff.
**Sue:** No, Shane! Please stay behind that partition and put on the charcoal suit as planned. I don't care what your friend sent you.
**Shane:** Too late; I'm already stepping into the trousers. You're gonna love this, Sue. It's a total vibe. The look is inspired by 80s popular shows. A complete homage to Miami Vice.
**Sue:** Miami Vice, Shane? It's 2026. We discussed timeless elegance, not vintage detective. Come out of there right now!
**Shane:** Tada! Awesome, right? I look so chic now. It's not too hot, so I won't be sweating under the flash bulbs. The colors are vibrant, and the fit is super relaxed.
**Sue:** You call this vibrant? It's a neon salmon shade. The fabric is practically see-through; the colours are just tacky if you don't mind me saying. And the fit — it feels so relaxed it must be at least three sizes too big for you. You look like you're melting in it.
**Shane:** My friend said this is the latest in male fashion. He said oversized is the new tailored.
**Sue:** Listen to me, Shane. You cannot go out like that. I simply won't let you. You're not even wearing socks.
**Shane:** Ah, that's because my ankles need to match my arms.
**Sue:** Do not pull up your jacket sleeves. Don't you dare!
**Shane:** Why not, Sue? It's breezy.
**Sue:** No, Shane. No, this is a disaster.
**Shane:** Come on! Is it really that bad?
**Sue:** The tabloids and the online haters will have **a field day** with this catastrophe that you call fashion. You'll be a meme before you even reach the theater. Now, please go back and get dressed with the suit the stylist sent you right now.
**Shane:** Fine, but for the record, you're killing my creative soul.
**Sue:** No. What you're killing is your career, and mine. If you dare step outside looking like an '80s lava lamp filled with glitter — move!

### Meaning
어떤 상황이나 기회를 최대한 이용하며 아주 신나게 즐기는 것. 크게 두 가지 뉘앙스:
1. **긍정적 의미**: 진짜로 재미있게 즐기는 것 (아이들이 놀이공원에서 신나게 노는 것 등)
2. **부정적/비꼬는 의미 (더 흔히 쓰임)**: 언론이나 비평가가 남의 실수나 스캔들을 신나게 공격/이용하는 상황. "딱 먹이감이 걸려들었네" 같은 느낌.

**Origin:** 1700년대 군대에서 유래. 병사들이 막사를 떠나 실제 들판(field)으로 나가 훈련과 기동 연습을 하는 특별한 날(field day)에서 비롯됨.

### Example Usages
- (긍정) Food lovers had a field day at the night market.
- (긍정) Investors had a field day as stock prices surged.
- (부정) The movie was so poorly acted that the film critics had a field day writing their sarcastic reviews.
- (부정) If the governor is caught lying, the news networks are gonna have a field day tearing his reputation apart.

### Similar Expressions
- **to go to town on somebody** — to criticize or attack someone enthusiastically
  - e.g. The media went to town on the political candidate.
- **ripped to shreds** — to tear apart in criticism
  - e.g. The media ripped the candidate to shreds.
- **to go wild** — to react with great enthusiasm or intensity

## 3. New York Times Article

- Title: **Migrating American Woodcocks Set Off a Birding Frenzy in Bryant Park**
- Korean title: 미국 맷도요, Bryant Park에서 조류 관찰 열풍을 일으키다
- Topic summary: 매년 봄 북쪽으로 이동하는 철새 미국 맷도요(American woodcock)가 뉴욕 맨해튼 미드타운 Bryant Park에 잠시 머물면서, 존 트로볼타의 Saturday Night Fever를 연상시키는 독특한 디스코풍 걸음걸이로 소셜미디어를 달구고 수많은 시민을 끌어모으고 있는 현상을 다룬 기사.
- Why it matters: 야생 동물과 도심 문화의 흥미로운 교차점을 보여주며, 소셜미디어가 자연 현상에 대한 관심을 얼마나 빠르게 확산시킬 수 있는지를 잘 보여주는 사례.

### Key Article Passages
1. The paparazzi staked out their turf, elbow to elbow, with dozens of other gawkers on their lunch break, on an unseasonably warm spring day in Bryant Park in Midtown Manhattan.
2. One celebrity visitor — an American woodcock, a migratory bird — bopped along in a manner befitting John Travolta in Saturday Night Fever.
3. Birders and non-birders alike have flocked to the park this week to catch a glimpse of the quirky visitors.
4. Videos of the boogieing birds, which are also known as timberdoodles, have flooded social media feeds in recent days, often with musical accompaniment: "Staying Alive" by The Bee Gees or "Rock That Body" by The Black Eyed Peas.
5. Serious birders are quick to follow etiquette, including discouraging people from flushing the birds out into the open for photos.

## 4. Vocabulary and Useful Sentences

### birding frenzy
- Meaning: uncontrollable excitement or popularity around birdwatching
- Korean: 조류 관찰 열풍
- Useful sentence: A birding frenzy swept through Bryant Park as the American woodcock made its annual appearance.
- Sentence Korean: 미국 맷도요가 매년 나타나면서 Bryant Park에 조류 관찰 열풍이 불었다.
- Note: **frenzy** = 통제할 수 없는 흥분/열광. "a shopping frenzy", "a media frenzy" 등 다양하게 활용 가능.

### stake something out
- Meaning: to closely monitor or watch a place over time; or to claim/occupy one's territory
- Korean: 감시하다, 자리를 차지하다, 영역을 표시하다
- Useful sentence: Fans staked out the hotel lobby hoping to catch a glimpse of the celebrity.
- Sentence Korean: 팬들은 유명인을 한 번이라도 보기 위해 호텔 로비를 지켜봤다.
- Note: "stake" = 땅에 박는 말뚝. 영역 표시에서 파생. "stake out" = 감시하다 / 자신의 자리나 의견을 주장하다.

### turf
- Meaning: one's territory, area of influence, or domain
- Korean: 자기 영역, 자신의 구역
- Useful sentence: The paparazzi staked out their turf, elbow to elbow, to photograph the woodcock.
- Sentence Korean: 파파라치들이 서로 어깨를 부딪히며 자신의 자리를 차지해 맷도요를 촬영했다.
- Note: 비격식 표현. 스포츠, 비즈니스, 일상 대화에서 폭넓게 쓰임.

### befitting
- Meaning: perfectly suitable or appropriate for someone or something
- Korean: ~에 걸맞은, ~에 어울리는
- Useful sentence: The woodcock bopped along in a manner befitting John Travolta in Saturday Night Fever.
- Sentence Korean: 그 새는 마치 Saturday Night Fever의 존 트로볼타를 연상시키는 방식으로 걸어다녔다.
- Note: 격식체. "befitting X" = "X에 꼭 맞는". "a reward befitting his talent" 처럼 쓸 수 있음.

### bop / boogie (shimmy)
- Meaning: to move rhythmically in a dance-like way, especially in a disco or up-tempo style
- Korean: 신나게 춤을 추듯 몸을 흔들다
- Useful sentence: The birds were boogieing along to the beat of "Staying Alive" in videos that flooded social media.
- Sentence Korean: 새들이 소셜미디어를 가득 채운 영상에서 'Staying Alive'에 맞춰 신나게 몸을 흔들었다.
- Note: **bop/boogie** = 신나게 춤추듯 움직이다. **shimmy** = 특히 어깨를 중심으로 좌우로 흔드는 동작.

### birder
- Meaning: a person who watches birds as a hobby; birdwatcher
- Korean: 조류 관찰자
- Useful sentence: Birders and non-birders alike have flocked to the park to catch a glimpse of the quirky visitors.
- Sentence Korean: 조류 관찰자든 아니든 모두가 이 별난 손님들을 한 번이라도 보려고 공원에 몰려들었다.
- Note: **birder** = birdwatcher의 비격식 표현. 서양에서는 새 관찰이 대중적 취미로 정착되어 있음.

### belly up to something
- Meaning: to move very close to something, pressing up against it
- Korean: 어디에 바짝 다가가다
- Useful sentence: Onlookers bellied up to the wrought iron fence to get a closer look at the woodcock.
- Sentence Korean: 구경꾼들은 철제 울타리에 바짝 붙어서 맷도요를 더 가까이 보려고 했다.
- Note: **go belly up** (파산하다)와 혼동 주의! "belly up to 장소" = 어디에 바짝 다가가다.

### hysterical
- Meaning: extremely funny (colloquial); originally means uncontrollably emotional
- Korean: (구어) 너무 웃기다 / 폭소를 자아내는
- Useful sentence: "It's hysterical," she said. "Every third TikTok video is the woodcock dance."
- Sentence Korean: "너무 웃겨요. TikTok에 올라오는 영상 세 개 중 하나는 이 새의 춤이에요."
- Note: 원래는 히스테리와 관련된 단어이지만, 구어에서는 "It's hysterical" = "너무 웃겨" 의미로 빈번히 사용됨.

### repeat visitor / repeat offender
- Meaning: someone who has returned again (visitor); someone who has committed an offense again (legal)
- Korean: 재방문객 / 상습범
- Useful sentence: Some of the birds in Bryant Park were believed to be repeat visitors from previous years.
- Sentence Korean: Bryant Park에 나타난 맷도요 중 일부는 이전 해에도 방문했던 새들로 추정되었다.
- Note: 형용사로 쓸 때 강세 위치가 달라질 수 있음 — **REpeat** visitor vs re**PEAT** (동사).

### shy away from something
- Meaning: to avoid something because you're hesitant, reluctant, or afraid
- Korean: ~을 피하다, ~에서 물러나다
- Useful sentence: Many investors are shying away from risky assets amid market uncertainty.
- Sentence Korean: 시장의 불확실성 속에서 많은 투자자들이 위험 자산을 피하고 있다.
- Note: "shy"가 동사로 쓰임. 과거형 = **shied** (shy → shied). 두려움이나 꺼림칙함 때문에 피하는 느낌.

### take cover
- Meaning: to move to a protected place to avoid danger
- Korean: 몸을 숨기다, 피난하다
- Useful sentence: The woodcock took cover in a bed of daffodils to escape the crowds.
- Sentence Korean: 그 맷도요는 군중을 피해 수선화 화단 속으로 몸을 숨겼다.
- Note: 군사적 상황("Take cover!")부터 일상적 상황(비를 피하다)까지 폭넓게 쓰임.

### crane one's neck
- Meaning: to stretch or twist the neck to see something better
- Korean: 목을 길게 빼다, 고개를 쭉 내밀다
- Useful sentence: Passengers craned their necks to look out the window as the plane descended.
- Sentence Korean: 비행기가 착륙하면서 승객들은 창밖을 보기 위해 목을 길게 뻗었다.
- Note: **crane** = 학(두루미). 긴 목을 뻗는 동작에서 동사로 파생됨.

### flush something/someone out of something
- Meaning: to force a person or animal to leave a hiding place or reveal itself
- Korean: 숨어있던 곳에서 몰아내다, 끌어내다
- Useful sentence: Police launched an operation to flush the suspect out of the building.
- Sentence Korean: 경찰은 용의자가 건물 밖으로 나오도록 하는 작전을 실행했다.
- Note: 변기를 flush하면 물이 강제로 흘러가는 것처럼, 무언가를 강제로 밖으로 몰아낼 때 사용. 벌레/동물/용의자 모두에 쓸 수 있음.

### give someone their space
- Meaning: to maintain physical or emotional distance so someone feels comfortable
- Korean: 공간을 주다, 적당한 거리를 유지하다
- Useful sentence: Park officials said that the crowds have mostly given the birds their space.
- Sentence Korean: 공원 관계자들은 방문객들이 대체로 새들에게 충분한 공간을 확보해줬다고 전했다.
- Note: 연인 간에는 "Give me some space." = "나만의 시간이 필요해." 로도 자주 쓰임.

## 5. Final Main Expressions Recap

1. **stake something out**
   - Meaning: to closely monitor or surveil a place; to claim one's spot/territory
   - Example: Fans staked out the hotel lobby hoping to catch a glimpse of the celebrity.
   - Korean: 감시하다 / 자신의 자리나 영역을 차지하다

2. **shy away from something**
   - Meaning: to avoid something due to hesitation, reluctance, or fear
   - Example: Many investors are shying away from risky assets amid market uncertainty.
   - Korean: ~을 피하다, ~로부터 물러서다

3. **crane one's neck**
   - Meaning: to stretch or extend the neck to see something better
   - Example: Passengers craned their necks to look out the window as the plane descended.
   - Korean: 목을 길게 빼다

4. **flush something/someone out of something**
   - Meaning: to force out of hiding; to make someone/something reveal itself
   - Example: Police launched an operation to flush the suspect out of the building.
   - Korean: ~에서 몰아내다, 끌어내다
