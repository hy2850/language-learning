# Kicking Things Off in Jeju

## 1. Warm-up

Min and Sarah have just arrived at their rental house in Jeju. After picking up a rental car, they decide how to begin their trip by checking out a seafood restaurant near the harbor. This lesson focuses on starting a trip, exploring a new place, and ordering everything available.

민과 사라는 제주도 렌털 하우스에 막 도착했습니다. 렌터카도 무사히 찾았고, 이제 항구 근처 해산물 식당부터 가 보며 여행을 시작하려고 해요. 이번 레슨에서는 여행을 시작하고, 새로운 장소를 둘러보고, 가능한 메뉴를 전부 즐기는 표현을 배웁니다.

---

## 2. Expressions

**1. kick things off**
- To start or begin something, especially an event, trip, project, or activity with a first step
- 시작하다, 첫 순서를 열다

**2. scope out**
- To look around or investigate a place, situation, or option before deciding what to do
- 미리 둘러보다, 살펴보다

**3. the works**
- Everything available; the full set of options, toppings, or services
- 가능한 건 전부, 풀 세트

## 5. Notes

### kick things off
- To kick things off means to start something with the first action or activity. It is often used for events, trips, meetings, shows, and projects.
- **A:** How should we start the welcome party?
  **B:** Let's kick things off with a quick toast.
- The conference will kick things off with a keynote speech at nine.

### scope out
- To scope out something means to look around, check it carefully, or investigate it before making a decision. It is useful when exploring restaurants, neighborhoods, hotels, campuses, or travel spots.
- **A:** Should we eat at the first cafe we see?
  **B:** Let's scope out the street first and compare a few places.
- Before booking the apartment, we scoped out the neighborhood at night.

### the works
- The works means everything available or the full set of options. It is common with food, spa services, car cleaning, and other packages where you can choose add-ons.
- **A:** What do you want on your burger?
  **B:** Give me the works: cheese, onions, pickles, and sauce.
- She booked a spa day with the works after a stressful month.

## 6. Power Pattern

### I know
- Used to show that you strongly agree with someone or feel the same way. The warm, rising intonation is important because it sounds empathetic, not dismissive.
- 맞아, 그러게, 내 말이

**Examples:**
1. This view is amazing! I know. We're so lucky to be here.
2. You look exhausted. I know; I barely slept last night.

## 7. Power Vocabulary

### breeze
- Something that is a breeze is very easy to do or accomplish. It feels simple and stress-free.
- 아주 쉬운 일, 식은 죽 먹기

**Examples:**
1. Picking up the rental car was a breeze because there was no line.
2. The online reservation was a breeze, so we finished it in five minutes.
