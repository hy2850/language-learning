## 2. Today's Idiom
- Idiom: the cat's pajamas
- Korean meaning: 아주 끝내주는 것, 최고인 것

### Setup
- 햄버거 가게에서 키오스크 주문을 어려워하는 70대 고객을 아르바이트생이 도와주는 상황.
- Che Soo Jin은 아르바이트생 역할, Paul Matthews는 고객 역할을 맡음.

### Dialogue
**Worker:** Excuse me, sir. You look like you could use a hand. Would you like some help with the kiosk?

**Customer:** Oh yes, please. I've been standing here for ten minutes and I still can't figure out how to order a simple burger.

**Worker:** No problem at all. Let's do it together. First tap "start order."

**Customer:** Like this?

**Worker:** Perfect. Now what kind of burger would you like?

**Customer:** I'll have a cheeseburger.

**Worker:** Single or double?

**Customer:** Let's go with a single.

**Worker:** Great. Now it's asking if you'd like your burger in a set.

**Customer:** I think so.

**Worker:** Wonderful. Next, what size fries would you like? It says small, medium, or large.

**Customer:** Medium sounds about right.

**Worker:** Excellent choice. Now for your drink, we have Coke, Zero Sugar Coke, lemon-lime soda, iced tea, orange juice, coffee, and bottled water.

**Customer:** I think I'll take an iced tea.

**Worker:** All right, good choice. Now it's asking if you'd like to add something else, like nuggets or a salad.

**Customer:** Nope. I think that's enough.

**Worker:** Okay, we're almost done. How about dessert?

**Customer:** I didn't know they had dessert.

**Worker:** We have apple pie, baked sweet potato churros, and mini patbingsu.

**Customer:** Mini patbingsu? That sounds refreshing.

**Worker:** It's actually one of our most popular desserts.

**Customer:** Then I'll try one.

**Worker:** Excellent choice. Now let's review your order: one cheeseburger meal, medium fries, an iced tea with ice, and one mini patbingsu. That looks right?

**Customer:** Great.

**Worker:** Please tap confirm.

**Customer:** Confirm. Done.

**Worker:** All right. Now you can pay with your credit card right here.

**Customer:** That was much easier than I expected.

**Worker:** See? It's not so bad, right?

**Customer:** Not bad at all. I actually learned a lot today. Maybe technology isn't as scary as I thought.

**Worker:** I'm glad to hear that.

**Customer:** It reminds me of when I bought my very first computer back in the 90s. I was nervous then too. Back then I thought that computer was the cat's pajamas, but it's got nothing on this kiosk.

**Worker:** The cat's pajamas?

**Customer:** Yes, the cat's pajamas is an old expression. It means something is the best.

**Worker:** I love that. I've never heard of it before.

**Customer:** Most people your age probably haven't.

**Worker:** I wonder why. It's very catchy.

**Customer:** Oh no, pickles. I didn't want pickles.

**Worker:** Oh no. We'll have to do it right from the start again. Let's cancel everything.

### Meaning
- Something is excellent, outstanding, stylish, or "the best."
- 오래된 표현으로, 어떤 사람이나 물건이 아주 멋지고 훌륭하다는 뜻.

### Host Explanation Notes
- Paul explained that the expression goes back to the 1920s in the United States.
- At that time, "cat" could mean a cool, trendy person, and pajamas were becoming fashionable.
- The image is "a cool person wearing cool clothes," so the phrase came to mean "the best of the best."
- The hosts noted that it is old-fashioned but catchy.

### Example Usages
- The band that was playing was absolutely the cat's pajamas. They were so good.
- You need to try this new coffee shop down the street. Their iced lattes are the cat's pajamas.
- Just because you're the host of the show, do you think you're the cat's pajamas?

### Similar Expressions

- the cream of the crop
- creme de la creme
- a cut above the rest
- second to none
- the GOAT (mainly associated with sports, according to Paul)

## 4. Vocabulary and Useful Sentences

### machete
- Meaning: a large knife with a wide blade, used for cutting through thick plants or forest.
- Korean: 마체테, 날이 넓은 큰 칼
- Useful sentence: That left walking, machetes in hand and boots in the mud, for three more miles to the site.
- Sentence Korean: 그들은 마체테를 손에 들고 진흙 속에 발을 디딘 채 유적지까지 3마일을 더 걸어가야 했다.
- Note: Paul explained that a machete is a big knife used to clear branches and plants in a forest.

### off the beaten path
- Meaning: not well known, not mainstream, or physically far from common roads and paths.
- Korean: 사람들의 발길이 닿지 않은, 잘 알려지지 않은
- Useful sentence: The cafe I'm taking you to is a little off the beaten path, but it's well worth a visit.
- Sentence Korean: 내가 데려가려는 카페는 조금 숨은 곳에 있지만, 가볼 만한 가치가 충분해.
- Note: In the article, it is both literal, because the site was deep in the jungle, and figurative, because it was unknown.

### cleave
- Meaning: to cut or split something with strong force.
- Korean: 쪼개다, 가르다, 베어 길을 내다
- Useful sentence: They drove down an old forest road and cleaved a path for ATVs.
- Sentence Korean: 그들은 낡은 숲길을 따라가며 ATV가 지나갈 수 있도록 길을 베어 냈다.
- Note: Paul connected "cleave" with "cleaver" and "machete," emphasizing forceful cutting.

### relief
- Meaning: a carving method where the design stands out from the surface.
- Korean: 부조, 양각
- Useful sentence: On one monument, a relief was carved depicting a scene of decapitation.
- Sentence Korean: 한 기념물에는 참수 장면을 묘사한 부조가 새겨져 있었다.
- Note: The hosts clarified that this is not "relief" meaning comfort or easing pain.

### bear / bore a date
- Meaning: to carry, display, or have a mark, date, or inscription on a surface.
- Korean: 날짜나 표식이 새겨져 있다, 지니다
- Useful sentence: Another monument bore a date in the late 600s A.D.
- Sentence Korean: 또 다른 기념물에는 서기 600년대 후반의 날짜가 새겨져 있었다.
- Note: Paul explained "it bears a date" as meaning the date is on that object.

### antiquity
- Meaning: an old object or artifact, especially from ancient times.
- Korean: 고대 유물
- Useful sentence: The black market for antiquities was nowhere near as developed as it is now.
- Sentence Korean: 고대 유물 암시장은 오늘날만큼 전혀 발달해 있지 않았다.
- Note: Paul compared "antiquity" with "antique," meaning something very old.

### nowhere near
- Meaning: not close to something; not at all similar or not at all at that level.
- Korean: 전혀 미치지 못하는, 근처에도 못 가는
- Useful sentence: I'm nowhere near as fit as I was when I was eighteen. I need to exercise more.
- Sentence Korean: 나는 열여덟 살 때만큼 전혀 건강하지 않아. 운동을 더 해야겠어.
- Note: In the article, the phrase describes how much less developed the antiquities black market was 70 or 80 years ago.

### without a trace
- Meaning: with no evidence, sign, or clue left behind.
- Korean: 흔적도 없이
- Useful sentence: He seems to have disappeared without a trace.
- Sentence Korean: 그는 흔적도 없이 사라진 것 같다.
- Note: Paul explained it as meaning there is no evidence something was ever there.

### a prelude to something
- Meaning: a smaller event or opening stage that comes before a bigger event.
- Korean: -의 서막, 전조
- Useful sentence: The invention of the radio was a prelude to the enormous technological explosion that was to come a little later.
- Sentence Korean: 라디오의 발명은 이후에 찾아올 거대한 기술 폭발의 서막이었다.
- Note: Paul connected the word to classical music, where a prelude comes before the main piece.

### deface
- Meaning: to damage or spoil the surface or appearance of something.
- Korean: 외관을 훼손하다
- Useful sentence: Defacing monuments in rival settlements was meant to erase and destroy the political and social memory.
- Sentence Korean: 경쟁 정착지의 기념물을 훼손하는 것은 정치적, 사회적 기억을 지우고 파괴하려는 목적이었다.
- Note: The hosts used examples such as graffiti or tourists carving names into monuments.

### understudied
- Meaning: not studied enough or not researched as much as it should be.
- Korean: 충분히 연구되지 않은
- Useful sentence: The discoveries were leading to insights about a long-understudied part of Mexico.
- Sentence Korean: 그 발견은 오랫동안 충분히 연구되지 않았던 멕시코의 한 지역에 대한 통찰을 가져오고 있었다.
- Note: Paul warned not to confuse the adjective "understudied" with the noun "understudy," an actor prepared to replace another actor.

### blind spot
- Meaning: an area you cannot see or a subject you are not aware of.
- Korean: 사각지대, 인식하지 못하는 영역
- Useful sentence: As a cyclist, you should always be aware that you could be in a motorist's blind spot.
- Sentence Korean: 자전거 운전자는 자신이 자동차 운전자의 사각지대에 있을 수 있다는 점을 항상 유의해야 한다.
- Note: The article uses "archaeological blind spot" for a region that had been hard for researchers to recognize or study.

### meal / combo meal
- Meaning: a fast-food order that includes a main item, side, and drink.
- Korean: 세트 메뉴
- Useful sentence: I'll have the cheeseburger meal.
- Sentence Korean: 치즈버거 세트로 할게요.
- Note: The hosts explained that in many English-speaking fast-food settings, "meal" or "combo meal" is more natural than saying "set one."
